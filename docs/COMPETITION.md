# Court Duel, ratings and rankings

A one-versus-one basketball mode built on the existing ball physics. The room server runs the
whole match; clients only ask to challenge, accept, decline or concede.

## Rules (shown in the game)

1. Two players on The Courts. One challenges (from the basketball panel's **Court Duel** section);
   the other accepts within 30 seconds.
2. A 3-second countdown. Leaving the court or disconnecting during it cancels the match with no
   result.
3. Each player is handed their own ball. The two court balls are **reserved** for the match: nobody
   else can pick them up or reset them, and the owner can retrieve theirs.
4. 60 seconds of play. A basket counts when it goes in from the player's own ball **and** the shot
   was released from inside the court: 2 points, 3 from beyond 6.75 m (the existing scoring).
5. The buzzer: a counting shot still in the air at the end counts if it drops within 3 seconds.
6. Highest score wins; equal scores draw.
7. Leaving the court for more than 6 seconds, conceding, or disconnecting during play forfeits.
8. One duel at a time (the court has two balls). Free shooting resumes when it ends. Players keep
   their result on screen, with **Rematch** and **Rankings**, until they close it.

Spectators near the court see the live scoreboard.

## Why scores cannot be faked

* Clients send `shoot` requests with a facing and a charge, exactly as in free play; the room runs
  the ball physics and emits `score` events.
* A score counts only if the room recorded that shot id as released by that player, with their
  own match ball, from inside the court. Each eligible shot can score once.
* Match state, the clock, forfeits and the winner are computed by the room
  (`server/duels.mjs`). Clients never submit a score.

## Recording, ratings and the leaderboard

Duels between two different signed-in players are recorded by the room through
`record_duel()` (service role only; `supabase/migrations/20261014120000_duels_and_achievements.sql`).

* **Idempotent**: the room assigns the match id; a repeated or concurrent submission of the same id
  is reported as a duplicate and changes nothing (tested with five simultaneous submissions). The
  room retries up to three times with backoff if the account service is unavailable.
* **Ratings**: everyone starts at 1000. After a rated duel both ratings move by
  `round(24 × (result − expected))` in opposite directions, where result is 1 / ½ / 0 and
  `expected = 1 / (1 + 10^((opponent − player) / 400))` (Elo, K = 24).
* **Anti-farming**: a duel is unrated if the same two players already played 3 rated duels in the
  previous 24 hours, or if it ended by forfeit within its first 10 seconds. Unrated duels still count
  as played, won and lost.
* **Leaderboard** (`duel_leaderboard()`): players with at least 3 rated duels, by rating. Equal
  ratings share a rank; within a tie, more wins first, then handle. Players with fewer than 3 rated
  duels are counted ("N players have played but are not ranked yet"), not ranked. It reads only
  recorded results.
* **History** on profiles: won–lost–drawn, rating once ranked, and recent duels.
* Badges: **Good Game** (finish a duel against a signed-in player) and **Court Champion** (3 rated
  wins against at least 2 different opponents, which also unlocks a gold accent colour).

Guest duels are fully playable but not recorded, and the result says so.

## Tests

* `tests/duels.test.mjs`: a full duel scored by real physics (free throws), ball reservation,
  only-one-duel, eligibility rules, concede, leaving the court, disconnects, cancellation, expiry and
  declines, and recording with retries carrying one match id.
* `tests/competition.test.mjs`: service-only recording, concurrency, Elo values replayed against the
  published formula, draws, forfeits, the pair limit, Court Champion, leaderboard ordering and ties.
* `tests/browser-duel.mjs`: two signed-in players in real browsers: game modes on the court,
  challenge and accept, balls handed over, live scoreboard, concede, the rated result shown to both
  and stored in the database, rankings and history.

## Not built (yet)

* **Team play (2v2).** The physics supports it, but possession, passing and fair scoring rules for
  teams need design and testing first.
* **Tournament brackets.** The *Basketball tournament night* event template schedules a session at
  The Courts where duels are played and rated, but there is no automatic bracket. The intended design:
  a `tournaments` table linked to an event, registration from RSVPs, a single-elimination bracket
  generated by the organizer when check-in closes, each pairing played as a Court Duel whose match
  id is linked to the bracket slot, no-shows advancing the opponent after a timeout, and champions
  recorded on profiles. It should be built only after plain duels have been used in practice.
* Defence (steals, blocks): the current ball model attaches a held ball to its holder.
