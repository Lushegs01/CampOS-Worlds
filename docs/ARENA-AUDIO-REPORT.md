# Combat Arena: audio overhaul

This report covers the replacement of Combat Arena's audio: what was there, what replaced it, how it was built,
how it was tested and what the tests measured, and what is still not done. Every number comes from a script in
this repository that can be re-run.

**Test environment.** A Linux container (4 cores, no GPU, no sound card) with headless Chromium. The tests read the
arena's audio graph directly: every sound the engine starts (name, time, level, position, occlusion) and the mixed
output through an analyser after the limiter. **Nobody listened to these sounds during this work**: there is no
audio output in this environment, so quality checks are measurements (level, spectrum, onset, clipping, noise floor,
timing), not listening tests. No physical phones, tablets or headphones were available; device behaviour (HRTF on
desktop, stereo on phones, small-speaker filtering, interruption recovery) was tested in emulation only.

Details: [pipeline](audio/README.md) · [inventory](audio/INVENTORY.md) · [sources and licences](audio/LICENSES.md) ·
[QC per clip](audio/qc.json) · results [phone suite](arena-audio-results.json), [match suite](arena-audio-match-results.json),
[weapon measurements](performance/arena-sounds.json), [soak](performance/arena-soak.json).

## What was there

The arena had no recorded weapon, impact, voice or interface sound. `src/arena/sound/designs.ts` synthesised every
gun (filtered noise and resonators, eleven parameter sets), every reload click, impact, flyby, explosion and Foley
sound in the browser at load time; oscillator tones served as UI sounds and hit markers. Footsteps were three campus
recordings (paving, grass, timber) pitched for other surfaces, fired on a timer; there were no hit reactions, no
voices, no announcer, no breathing and no map ambience beyond the campus wind. Reload sounds were three timed clicks
per weapon family (`setTimeout` at 18 %, 60 % and 84 % of the reload), not tied to the animation and not cancelled
when the reload was.

## What replaced it

### Recordings and licences

All sound is now edited from real recordings under licences that allow use and modification in a game, documented
per recording in [LICENSES.md](audio/LICENSES.md) (generated by the build from what is actually used):

| Source | Licence | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| The Free Firearm Sound Library (24-bit/96 kHz field recordings of real firearms, several microphone positions) | CC0 | Every gunshot: close body, outdoor tail, mid and far microphones |
| Gary, *Handling Guns* (real pistol, rifle and shotgun mechanisms, 16-bit WAV) | CC BY-SA 3.0 | Magazines, bolts, slides, pumps, hammers, cylinder, shells |
| BigSoundBank (Joseph Sardin; professional field and Foley recordings) | CC0 | Footsteps, landings, cartridge cases on concrete (9 mm, 5.56, 7.62), body falls, Foley, impacts, explosions, ambience, breathing |
| congusbongus (from freesound recordings by swuing, Ali_6868, Eelke), Fantozzi, HaelDB | CC BY 3.0 / CC0 | Footsteps by surface, gear and armour Foley |
| VoiceBosch (SoundBiter), Michel Baradari, HaelDB, AuraVoice | CC BY-SA 4.0 / CC BY 3.0 / CC0 | Soldier voices (four male, one female), announcer and radio lines |
| Kenney, rubberduck, dklon | CC0 / CC BY 3.0 | Interface, impacts, breaking and debris, Foley |

No game audio, no commercial library and nothing of unclear origin is used. CC BY and CC BY-SA works are credited in
the game (Combat Arena lobby → **Sound credits**) with a note of the changes; the banks built from CC BY-SA recordings
are named in LICENSES.md and shared under the same licence. Each weapon's sound comes from a real firearm of a similar
class and calibre (e.g. Vanguard ← AR-15, Halcyon ← AK-47, Wasp ← Carl Gustav M/45, Longbow ← Tikka bolt action,
Thorn ← S&W 642 with a .357 Magnum layer); the recorded firearm is named in the bank's metadata and the inventory.

Where no recording of a weapon's own part existed, the nearest real mechanism was pitched for the part's size (e.g.
an LMG feed cover from a rifle action); a ricochet and a subsonic whiz were built from real whooshes and metal hits;
metal container footsteps layer real metal steps with a steel bar's ring. These are marked as designed in the
inventory. The one synthesised sound is the tinnitus ring after a flashbang (a fading pure tone).

### Pipeline

`scripts/assets/build-arena-audio.mjs` downloads the masters, repairs flat-topped peaks in the firearm recordings
(ffmpeg `adeclip`), cuts every clip from a recipe (`scripts/assets/arena-audio/recipes.mjs`) with onset detection,
trims silence, applies sin² fades, filters, pitch and layering, normalises by short-term loudness per sound, and packs
each bank into an MP3 sprite at the bank's own rate (48 kHz stereo for close-up shots, 32 kHz mono for mechanics,
impacts and footsteps, 24 kHz voices, 16 kHz ambience beds). Every clip is then decoded back from the MP3 and checked:
clipping (flat runs at the native rate), DC offset, abrupt start or end, late onset, noise floor, decoder overshoot.

**Result:** 36 banks, 47 files, 1,000 clips, 4.08 MB in total; **0 clips flagged**. 457 source recordings are used
(164 of them from BigSoundBank).

### Engine and events

`src/arena/sound/engine.ts` was rewritten for recorded banks on the game's shared AudioContext:

- **Loading on demand.** Banks load by need, never all at start: interface with the lobby; at deployment the
  loadout's weapons, movement, handling, impacts, explosives, announcer, the player's voice and breathing and the
  map's ambience; footstep banks for surfaces within reach; other weapons and voices when a player using them is
  seen. Loading never blocks deployment (banks arrive in priority order; anything not ready is skipped or stood in
  for by a same-class weapon). Decoded memory has a budget (32 MB phones, 96 MB elsewhere) with least-recently-used
  eviction that never drops the player's own banks.
- **Decoder delay.** Each sprite carries a one-sample marker; the engine measures the browser's MP3 decoder delay after
  decoding, so clip offsets are exact across browsers (0 ms measured in Chromium).
- **Voices.** 20 on phones, 32 elsewhere, with priorities (own shots and the announcer highest), stealing of the
  quietest lower-priority voice, per-emitter limits, culling beyond each event's range and de-duplication.
- **Space.** HRTF panning on desktop, equal-power on phones; distance curves per event kind, air absorption, speed of
  sound delay for distant events, occlusion by the map's walls (low-pass and level), indoor reverb send by the
  listener's roof cover, distance layering of gunfire (close body → mid microphone → far microphone).
- **Mix.** Category buses (weapons, movement, character, environment, explosions/impacts, interface, announcer) with
  their own volumes in arena Settings → Sound (an "Announcer and radio" slider was added), a glue compressor and a
  brick-wall limiter; own shots and near blasts duck the environment, announcer lines duck the environment.
- **Robustness.** A suspended context (phone call, tab in background) is resumed by the next sound or touch, with
  nothing queued meanwhile; the arena fades out when the page is hidden; banks are released when leaving the arena.

`src/arena/sound/events.ts` is the data-driven event table (category, priority, range, distance curve, voice limits,
send, variation per kind of sound); `src/arena/audio.ts` maps game events to it.

### Gameplay integration

- **Weapons.** Each shot plays the weapon's recorded body (stereo for the player), its tail, its mechanical layer and
  the spent case landing on what is underfoot (pistol, rifle, full-power rifle or shotgun hull, hard or soft ground).
  Others' shots are layered by distance and placed where the shooter is; supersonic rounds passing close crack,
  subsonic ones whiz. Pump and bolt actions cycle between shots on the weapon's cooldown, matching the animation.
- **Reloads** (`src/arena/sound/reloads.mjs`). One plan per weapon, shared by the first-person animation and the
  sound, at fractions of the simulation's reload timer. Tactical reloads keep the magazine (to a pouch) and do not
  work the action; reloads from empty drop the magazine on the ground and chamber a round the way that mechanism
  does: AR bolt catch, AK and SMG charging handle, LMG feed cover and belt then charging handle, pistol slide stop,
  bolt-action four-stroke bolt, revolver cylinder/ejector/speed loader, pump shotgun shell by shell. Each clip is
  aligned by its loudest moment to the animation event. Switching weapons or dying stops the remaining parts. Other players' reloads are heard from their position, part by part (the server now reports whether a reload
  is from empty).
- **Footsteps** (`gait.mjs`, `surfaces.mjs`). The gait phase drives the camera bob, the viewmodel bob and the
  footfalls together, so a step is heard when the foot lands. Ten surfaces (concrete, asphalt, gravel, dirt, grass,
  sand, metal, container steel, wood, stone/tile) read from the same layout as the ground shader and the map's boxes;
  stairs have their own steps. Walk, run and crouch (soft) sets, scuffs on stops and sharp turns, landings by fall
  speed, slides on hard and soft ground, clothing, webbing and gear rattle by loadout weight, breathing after
  sprinting and at low health. Other soldiers' steps come from their animated stride in the snapshots.
- **Hit reactions** (`reactions.mjs`). Only from validated damage (the server's hit and damage events; health in
  snapshots for others), never predicted hits. The impact is on the helmet, plate carrier or cloth where the server
  says the round struck (the server now reports head/leg and the cover struck). Light, heavy and severe reactions by
  damage and remaining health; hits within 100 ms merge into one reaction; a minimum gap stops stammering; one
  elimination sound per life, then the body falling. The player's own reactions are quieter, with the impact placed
  toward the attacker.
- **Bullet impacts.** At the server-confirmed hit position, by surface (concrete, brick, steel, sheet metal, wood,
  dirt, sand, foliage; glass is mapped for maps that have it), with an occasional ricochet off steel.
- **Everything else.** Grenades (pin, spoon, throw, bounces by surface, blasts by distance, debris), smoke, flash
  (with the world dulled and the ring), equipment beeps, supply crates, air-strike siren, resupply, melee (draw,
  swing, strike per weapon), respawn, map ambience (wind, district, room tone indoors, machinery and electrical hum at
  placed emitters, occasional creaks, clanks, chains and flags), announcer (countdown, go, objectives secured, lost
  and contested, losing, a minute left, victory, defeat, draw, streaks, UAV, supply, strikes) and interface sounds
  (buttons, hover, tabs, toggles, sliders, errors, equip, purchase, hit and kill markers, medals, notifications).

### Removed

`src/arena/sound/designs.ts` and `src/arena/sound/dsp.ts` (the synthesis) after confirming nothing references them.
The campus recordings the arena used (`public/assets/audio/step-*.mp3`, `wind.mp3`) are still used by the campus and
were kept.

## Tests and results

All runs below are on the final build (36 banks, 0 QC flags).

| Test | What it does | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `node --test tests/arena-audio.test.mjs` | Reload plans for every weapon (order, tactical vs empty, cancellation points), gait cadence and phase, surfaces under positions (asphalt lanes, gravel margins, dirt patches, boxes, stairs), reaction rules (merge, gap, severity, one death per life), body part from hit height, shuffle bags never repeat | 13 / 13 pass |
| `npm test` | The whole unit and integration suite | 322 / 322 pass |
| `node tests/browser-arena-audio.mjs` (phone profile: 20 voices, stereo, 32 MB) | Plays the real game in Chromium | 16 / 16 checks pass |
| `node tests/browser-arena-audio-match.mjs` (desktop: 32 voices, HRTF) | A Training match against the server's bots; everything from the server | 5 / 5 checks pass |
| `CYCLES=3 node tests/browser-arena-soak.mjs` | Repeated matches, then leaving the arena | pass |
| `node scripts/qa/arena-sounds.mjs` | Measures every weapon's built reports | written |

Measured in the phone suite:

- **Loading.** 17 banks ready 1.0 s after deployment (the player could already play), decoded at their own rates,
  decoder delay 0 ms. Decoded memory then was 32.8 MB, 0.8 MB over the phone budget for a few seconds (seven
  footstep banks near the spawn were all within reach); the budget's eviction brought it back under (29.7 MB after a
  firefight with every weapon in the game heard, 20 banks let go and reloaded on demand).
- **Own gunfire.** The Vanguard's recorded body, its tail at +110 ms, the 5.56 case landing at +373 ms.
- **Tactical reload.** grab → magRelease → magOut → pouch → magIn → magSeat → settle; each part's loudest moment
  within −1…+2 ms of the animation reaching it (frame tolerance 60 ms). No bolt sound, no dropped magazine.
- **Reload from empty.** grab → magRelease → magOut → pouch → magDrop → magIn → magSeat → boltRelease → settle; the
  bolt 0 ms from the animation.
- **Interrupted reload.** Switching weapons at 40 %: magazine-in and seat never played; the switch and the
  secondary's draw were heard.
- **Footsteps.** 7 steps, one every 394 ms while walking, on asphalt then concrete as the player crossed them, with 3
  clothing and gear layers; silence when the player stopped.
- **Hit reactions.** Two hits 40 ms apart → one heavier grunt; helmet, plate carrier and cloth impacts each distinct;
  one elimination (a female soldier's voice, then the body falling); the player's own hit grunt quieter.
- **Remote reload.** An AK-pattern reload from empty from another player's position, ending with the charging handle.
- **Firefight stress.** 3 s with 12 shooters of every weapon, the player's automatic fire, explosions and impacts:
  peak −5.6 dBFS, 0 clipped samples (100 % of the output inspected), at most 20 voices, 767 calls (591 played, 750
  skipped by culling and limits, 169 voices stolen), 0.115 ms per call.
- **Distance.** At 4 m the close body (0.68) and tail; at 45 m mostly the mid microphone (0.30, body 0.02); at 130 m
  only the far microphone (0.25).
- **Occlusion.** The engine agreed with an independent line-of-sight check on 8 points around the player (4 open, 4
  walled off); the warehouse interior counted as indoors.
- **Ambience.** 5 loops running (wind, district, room tone and the nearest machines).
- **Volumes.** Weapons at 0 silenced the player's shot in the arena mix (−95 dBFS), at 1 it was back (−6.7 dBFS); the
  announcer played on its own bus.
- **Interruption.** A suspended context resumed by the next sound in 50 ms, with nothing queued.

Measured in the match suite (20 s against bots): 61 enemy reports from five weapons (Longbow, Wasp, Bastion, Anvil,
Vanguard) placed and layered by distance; 73 enemy footsteps on asphalt, gravel, concrete and dirt; 10 body impacts,
3 vocal reactions and 4 eliminations (each followed by a body fall); the player fired 11 rounds with 5 hit markers;
output peak −5.4 dBFS, RMS −28.7 dBFS, 0 clipped samples, at most 25 voices.

Soak (desktop, 3 matches then leaving): heap growth 1.3 MB over 3 matches; decoded audio 32–35 MB between matches
(within the 96 MB desktop budget) and **0 KB after leaving the arena**; no voices left playing.

Weapon measurements: every weapon's reports are measurably distinct from every other's (closest pair Bastion/Rampart,
both 12-gauge, distance 0.283; most distinct Kestrel/Longbow, 1.0); the loudest built clip peaks at −0.7 dBFS.

## Not done, and limits

- **No listening and no real devices.** Levels, balance and character were set by measurement and by the recordings'
  own quality, not by ear. A listening pass on headphones, a laptop and a phone speaker is the most important next
  step, especially for the relative level of footsteps against gunfire and the reverb amount indoors.
- **Masters.** BigSoundBank recordings are the site's free Ogg Vorbis downloads (~240 kb/s), not its WAV masters
  (those need an account). The firearm recordings are full-resolution WAV.
- **Not every part is that weapon's own recording.** Where a part of a weapon was not recorded, a real mechanism of
  the nearest type was used and pitched; this is listed per sound in the inventory.
- **Features the game does not have** were not given sounds: suppressors, malfunctions, weapon inspection,
  pickups of dropped weapons, doors and interactive objects in the arena maps.
- **Phone memory at deployment** can briefly exceed the 32 MB budget by about 1 MB when many footstep surfaces are
  near the spawn, until idle banks are let go (a few seconds).
- **Soak** was run for 3 matches this time (the script's default is 5).
